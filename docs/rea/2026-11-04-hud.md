# HUD: how the original reads its data (2026-11-04)

The HUD is defined by retail data. This note records how `MaxPayne.exe` and `e2mfc.dll` read it, so the engine
reads the same files the same way. Addresses are PE i386, ImageBase `0x400000` (`e2mfc.dll`: `0x10000000`).

## Files

- **VERIFIED:** `data/hud/hud.txt` (string `0x878f74`) lays out everything in 640x480 space. `MaxPayne_HUDMode` loads
  it: the keys are registered near `0x4adad4` to `0x4ade8c`, and the sections load from `0x4b13f4` to `0x4b20b8`.
- **VERIFIED:** `data/hud/bitmaps/` is in `x_data.ras`. `data/hud/bitmaps/weapons/` (35 files) is in the localized
  `x_<lang>.ras`, because the weapon names are in the images. The `+` between the ammo counts is in those images too.
- **VERIFIED:** the crosshair is not in `hud.txt`. Each weapon's `.txt` names a mesh under `[Crosshair]`:
  `database/weapons/crosshairs/dot.kf2` (13 weapons) or `invisible.kf2` (4), with `[CrosshairAttributes]` from
  `database/shooting.h`. The binary has an `X_Crosshair` class and the `C_DisplayCrosshair` message.

## `MaxPayne_HUDMode` members (VERIFIED, loader and update)

| Member | Holds |
|---|---|
| `+0xfc` | `[Health]` `[Sprite]`, the pain fill |
| `+0x100` | `[Health]` `[BackgroundSprite]` |
| `+0x104` | `[Health]` `[RegenerationSprite]` |
| `+0x108`, `+0x10c` | `[SlowMotion]` `[Sprite]`, `[BackgroundSprite]` |
| `+0x110`, `+0x114` | `AmmoInPocketText`, `AmmoInClipsText` |
| `+0x12c`, `+0x13c`, `+0x14c` | Hourglass colours |
| `+0x19c`, `+0x1a0` | `LowHealth`, `ShowInverseHealth` |

Sprites carry a value at `+0x110` set by the loader (regeneration 5, background 3). HYPOTHESIS: draw order.

## Update, `0x4aee20` (VERIFIED)

**Hourglass.** With `g` the slow-motion level (`0x421ae0`), the foreground's height is `height * g` and the top of
its texture coordinates is `1 - g`, so it shows the lower part of the bitmap. Both sprites are refreshed every
frame. The colour is `+0x13c` while active (`0x421af0`). Otherwise it is `+0x12c` when the level is above `0x421b00`,
else `+0x14c`. STRONGLY SUPPORTED: those are `ColorActive`, `ColorNormal` and `ColorUnavailable`, as the slots
follow the three colour loads at `0x4b171b`.

**Health.** With `h` the normalized health (`0x421e90`) and `r` the pending regeneration (`0x421ea0`):

1. When `h <= LowHealth`, the background and the fill are refreshed. `LowHealth = 1.0`, so that is always.
2. `k = h`. With `ShowInverseHealth`, `k = min(h + r, 1)`, then `k = 1 - k`.
3. The fill's height is `height * k` and the top of its texture coordinates `1 - k`.
4. When `r > 0`, the regeneration sprite shows above it (`0x4af11a` on).

So at full health the fill has no height and only the pale figure shows, as in retail frames.

## Fonts (VERIFIED, `e2mfc.dll`)

`P_Font::validate` (`0x10001e60`) walks the colour bitmap's first row for 256 characters: it skips black pixels,
stores the start of each coloured run, and stores `run + 1` as the width (`+0x50 + code * 4`). The height (`+0x4c`) is
the bitmap's height minus 2. `P_Font::getViewportSize` (`0x10001dc0`) sums the widths with no spacing.

**Measured:** a texel is one unit of 640x480 space. In retail frame `original/0104`, a digit pitch of 8 is 24 px across
1920, and the bright rows of a digit span 29 px of 1080. `18` ends at x = 582 and `99` starts at 597, on the anchors.

## Player weapons

- **VERIFIED (data):** the level scripts hand out the player's weapons. `Part0_Level1` runs `player->c_removeallweapons(empty)`
  and `c_pickupweapon(painkiller)` first, then later `c_removeallweapons(beretta)`, `c_setinfiniteammo(beretta, false)`,
  `c_pickupweapon(painkiller)`, `c_pickupweapon(beretta)` and `c_pickupammo(beretta, 99)`.
- **VERIFIED (docs):** `messages.htm` gives `C_RemoveAllWeapons(%s)`, `C_PickupWeapon(%s)`, `C_PickupAmmo(%s, %d)` and
  `C_SetInfiniteAmmo(%s, %d)`.
- **STRONGLY SUPPORTED (community tutorials `npc-helper`, `npc-helper2`):** `C_RemoveAllWeapons(x)` strips the character and
  gives `x` with unlimited ammo; `Empty` gives nothing. That matches `hud.txt`'s `UnlimitedAmmoText = "-"` and explains
  the house script's `c_setinfiniteammo(beretta, false)`.
- **VERIFIED (data):** each `database/weapons/<name>.txt` has `WeaponID`, `Clipsize` and `Pocketsize`, the sizes
  mostly through `shooting.h` (`BERETTA_CLIPSIZE = 18`, `BERETTA_POCKETSIZE = 162`).
- **VERIFIED (data):** `database/level_items/*.txt` are the pickups. A weapon's `[OnFirstPickup]`, `[OnPickUp]` and
  `[OnCantPickup]` lists hold the messages, and ammo comes only from their `C_PickupAmmo`, a fixed `PICKUPAMMO_*` from
  `level_items.h` (Beretta 10, Ingram 25). `C_PickupWeapon` itself adds none, which also fits the house's `18 + 99`.
- **VERIFIED (data), dual weapons:** `ingram.txt` gives the single weapon on `[OnFirstPickup]`, adds `ingramdual` on
  `[OnPickUp]`, and `[OnCantPickup]` still grants it at full ammo. `beretta.txt` gives both at once, because the
  player always holds one by then. Single and dual share an `InventoryID`, so they share rounds.
- **VERIFIED (data), reload:** each `CHARANIM_RELOAD<WEAPON>` in `default_skeleton.txt` sends `this->C_Reload()` at a
  frame, and the clip's frame rate gives the time: Beretta 39 at 30 fps, 1.30 s; Ingram 59 at 60 fps, 0.98 s.
  `[Revert]` lines reuse animations: the sniper takes the MP5's, the molotov the grenade's. `ReloadAmount` is -1 to
  fill the clip or a round count: pump shotgun 1.
- **VERIFIED (data), melee:** `baseballbat.txt` sends `C_SetInfiniteAmmo(baseballbat, true)`. Melee has no ammo rule.
- **Owner, from play:** a pickup only switches to a stronger weapon. `weaponpriority.txt` `[BestWeapons]` is that order;
  its comment adds "when running out of ammo". Weapons outside the list are not switched to.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** a one-round reload repeats until the clip is full. Weapon drops from enemies are unread.
- **Not done:** the player's reload animation does not play yet, only its timing. Level items cannot be picked up yet.

## Ammo and painkillers, `0x4afcf0` (VERIFIED)

- The weapon's `[ActiveWeapon]` entry is looked up by weapon ID and its sprite refreshed each frame.
- Both ammo numbers print with `%d` (`0x8698d0`). With infinite ammo (`0x421f70`), `UnlimitedAmmoText` goes in the
  pocket text and the clip text is cleared. The `+` and the weapon's name are in its bitmap.
- With a positive painkiller count (`0x421f20`) the count prints and the icon refreshes; at zero both are hidden.
  STRONGLY SUPPORTED: the count is the pocket, since the house gives one with `c_pickupweapon` and retail shows none.

## Slow motion (VERIFIED, data)

`globalconfig.txt` `[slowmotion]`: `SlowMotionTime = 15` seconds for a full gauge, `Threshold = 0.1%` to start.
HYPOTHESIS: the gauge starts full, drains in real time, and `RegenerationTime = 100000000` means it barely regenerates;
kills fill it (`BulletTimeGainedPerKillMultiplier` in `difficulty.txt`), which the engine does not do yet.

`HeartbeatSoundMessage` plays `menu\slomo_heartbeat.wav` every `HeartbeatSoundInterval = 1.25` seconds of slow motion. The engine
sends it from the start of slow motion. `[StartMessages]` (game speed, the `slow_motion` sound, a flash) are not run yet.

`RegenerationTime = 100000000` seconds ("extremely slow"), so resting barely refills the gauge. Kills do:
`SlowMotionBonusForKiller = BONUS_SLOWMOTION`, 0.12 of the gauge in `characters.h`. `BulletTimeGainedPerKillMultiplier` in
`difficulty.txt` scales it by difficulty; the engine does not apply that yet. HYPOTHESIS: the same for every enemy, though skins can set their own.

## Shots

- **Data:** `::StartRoom::lock` in `Tutorial.ldb` has `DO_BulletCollides` only in its state 2, which hides it and runs
  `Door.DO->DO_Animate(Open)`. The action trigger `T_Van` runs `lock->fsm_switch(2)`, so the padlock answers only once the
  tutorial reaches it. Level scripts own these doors.
- **Engine:** a shot runs `DO_BulletCollides` on the mesh it hits. It leaves from the camera through the dot, and stops at
  level geometry. The old `E` and missed-shot shortcuts, which toggled any mesh with an `open` track, are gone.
- **Not read:** how retail traces from aim to target (`DefaultAimingPointOffset`, `CastLength`).

## Bullet decals (VERIFIED, data)

`projectiles/bullet_beretta.txt` `[Decal]` sends `D_CreateDecal(bullethit_generic)`. That block of `decals/decals.txt` lists
`[NoDecalMaterial]` categories, two messages (a sound and a `bullethit_generic` particle effect), and a bitmap, alpha,
radius range, `RotationRandom` and `BitmapFlipRandom` per material. `globalconfig.txt` `[decal]` caps triangles: 2000 per room, 500 per dynamic object.

**Engine:** `decals.rs` places an oriented quad 1 cm off the surface a shot crosses, drawn through the sprite path, and
keeps 1000 in the whole level. A category with no entry takes `[Default]` (HYPOTHESIS). Not yet done: each weapon's own decal
block, the hit's sound and particles, clipping a decal at polygon edges, and following a dynamic mesh that moves.

## Printed text

`MPHM_Print*` messages name strings in `database/stringtable.txt` (`[ID] String = "text";`, Latin-1, in the localized archive).
`[Print]` and `[PrintTip]` in `hud.txt` give anchor, spacing, line count, colour and timing. `#USE#`-style placeholders are the
player's keys; the engine maps the four it has. The tutorial's `TIP_07` says to answer the phone and `TIP_12` to deal
with the padlock, which matches the script chain. HYPOTHESIS: right-anchored print grows upward, and long tips wrap
at about 620 units; neither is read.

## Bullet hits

`projectiles/bullet_beretta.txt` `[Decal]` runs `D_CreateDecal(bullethit_generic)`, and that `decals.txt` block carries two
`[Message]`s: `A_PlayFloating3DSound(projectiles, bullethit_generic)` and `PS_StartEffect(bullethit_generic)`. Both catalogs
give a `[Default]` and `[Material]` variants, so a wood hit sounds and looks like wood. `Filename` lists every
`[Sound]` of the variant. VERIFIED (data): `bullethit-*.rps` emit along local +y with Relative Position zero.
HYPOTHESIS: +y is the surface normal, and retail picks among the `[Sound]`s at random. Character hits run
`[CharacterHit]` instead (`P_CreateProjectile(blood_bullet_small, 1)`), which the engine does not do yet.

## Firing and reloading

The weapon file scripts its own shot: `WEAPONANIM_SHOOT` frame 0 runs `A_Play3DSound(weapons, shoot_beretta)`, a projectile and
`PS_StartEffect(Muzzle_Beretta)`, and `DefaultShootingFrequency` paces it. Max's body plays `CHARANIM_SHOOT<WEAPON>`, and a
reload plays `CHARANIM_RELOAD<WEAPON>`, whose `WS_Animate(WEAPONANIM_RELOAD)` frame starts the reload sound. The
clip plays whole while Max stands. While he runs, the bones `default_skeleton.txt` lists as `OneHandedUpperBodyBone` or
`TwoHandedUpperBodyBone` come from the clip and the rest from the run. HYPOTHESIS: retail also splits the body while he stands. The muzzle effect is anchored where it starts rather than following the `Gun` bone.

## Melee

`CHARANIM_SHOOTLEADPIPE` creates `bullet_leadpipe` from the neck at frames 7 and 31; the bat creates its projectile from the head.
`bullet_leadpipe.txt` flies at `Speed = 20` for `ExplosionDelay = 0.05`, then runs `P_Explosion(0.80, 0.801, 9, 8.99, ...)`.
That is 1 m of flight and, as a HYPOTHESIS for the first argument, 0.8 m of blast: 1.8 m of reach. The engine tests a ray
of that length from Max along the view at each blow. The engine has no `bullethit_leadpipe` decal yet, so a blow
leaves no mark, and its damage is the same single hit as a bullet.

## Deaths

`default_skeleton.txt` lists `CHARANIM_RANDOMDEATH1..4` (`death_minimum`, `death_stomach`, `death_staggerbackwards`, `death_headshot`).
`bullet_beretta.txt` says `DeathCause = PROJECTILEDEATH_RANDOM`. Each clip plays `A_Play3DSound(enemy, mobster_death)` at frame 0
and a body-fall sound when it lands. VERIFIED (data): all four end with the pelvis under 0.5 m. The body holds the last key,
because `sample_locals` wraps at the duration. `[Movement] EndPosition` is left unused: the clip's own pelvis carries the
fall, and the engine does not move the character. HYPOTHESIS: retail applies it only to where the character stands
afterwards. Still missing: shotgun and grenade deaths, blood, the death face texture and the dropped weapon.

## Level items

The `.ldb` places items by type, and `level_items/<type>.txt` gives each a model, `TriggerRadius`, `MatchingWeaponID`, the
`PickupIfNewWeapon` and `PickupIfAmmoNotFull` rules, and message lists `[OnFirstPickup]`, `[OnPickUp]` and `[OnCantPickup]`, whose
`activator` is the player. `CantPickupMessagesTimeout` paces the refusal. HYPOTHESES: "first" means the first of that type,
reach is `TriggerRadius` across and `CapsuleTop` upward, and an item's rotation is ignored because the `.ldb` reader keeps
only its position. `GM_ResetLevelItems` does nothing yet.

## Two-handed weapons

`TwoHandedAnimation = YES` (pump shotgun, MP5, sniper, M79, Jackhammer, bat) marks the weapons Max carries with the `CHARANIM_2_*`
set. `max_payne.txt` maps `CHARANIM_2_STAND` to `2_stand.kf2` and reverts `CHARANIM_2_WALK` onto `2_run.kf2`. The engine swaps
these two clips in. Strafes, jumps and crouches are not played for either stance, and `TwoHandedBones` is unread.

## Projectiles

Nothing about a shot lives in the engine. A weapon's `WEAPONANIM_SHOOT` lists its `P_CreateProjectile(name, count)` messages, and each
`projectiles/<name>.txt` gives `Damage`, `Accuracy` and `D_CreateDecal`, with numbers from `shooting.h`. The pump shotgun creates one
`bullet_shotgun_straight`, two `bullet_pumpshotgun` and two `bullet_pumpshotgun_group`: five rays of 5 damage. Each decal block
carries its own `[Message]`s, so `bullethit_shotgun` pellets are silent and `bullethit_shotgun_group` makes the sound and dust.

Rounds can vary inside one clip. `CHARANIM_SHOOTINGRAM` calls `WS_AnimateShooting` at frames 2, 5, 8 and 11, alternating `WEAPONANIM_SHOOT`
with `WEAPONANIM_SHOOT_NOEFFECTS`, whose `bullet_ingram_noeffects` uses the silent `bullethit_noeffects` decal. Each `WS_AnimateShooting` is one round, at
its own clip frame. STRONGLY SUPPORTED (data): `DefaultShootingFrequency` paces clip cycles, not rounds. The Ingram clip runs at 120 fps, so
its four rounds land between 17 and 92 ms, inside one 125 ms cycle. A new cycle restarts the clip and drops rounds it did not reach, which
matters for dual Ingrams (30 fps, eight rounds). HYPOTHESES: that restart, each round taking one from the clip, and held fire repeating
for every weapon, since no weapon file marks a gun automatic. `CHARANIM_SHOOTBERETTADUAL` fires right then left at frames 0 and 3.
`UseMultipleGunBones` puts a second gun on `LeftHandGunBone` (`hand-l`), and `TwoHandedBones`, not `TwoHandedAnimation`, picks the upper-body
set: the Ingram has the first without the second. `ShootingFrequencyMultiplier` is not applied yet. `bullet_sniper2x` and `bullet_sniper4x` are named
by no weapon or skeleton file read here, and `bullet_chaingun` by neither.

VERIFIED (`0x7068cd`, getter `0x574920` of member `+0x5e`, loaded from `Accuracy` at `0x5408e8`): the normalised aim gets a random
unit vector (`0x712870`) scaled by `rand() * Accuracy * 1.2207404e-8`, then is normalised again. With `rand()` up to 32767, that
reaches `Accuracy / 2500`. Enemies start at the skin's `MaximumHealth`, through `HEALTH_CLASS_*` in `ai_ranges.h`. No hit-location
or difficulty multiplier was found in the data. HYPOTHESES: `P_Explosion`'s third argument is a melee blow's damage, and
`rand()` stays uniform across the range.

## Engine status

`HudTarget::draw_hud` draws these. A frame rendered from the retail assets matches `original/0104` in place and size.
Health stays full until the player can be hurt. The HUD hides while a level camera owns the view; retail's
`MPHM_EnableHUD` and `MPHM_ChangeHUDVisibility` are not read yet.

## Crosshair

- **VERIFIED (data):** `[Crosshair]` in each weapon file names `dot.kf2` (13) or `invisible.kf2` (4), and the skins send
  `C_DisplayCrosshair`. `[CrosshairAttributes]` load at `0x53b5e7` into the weapon's data.
- **Measured (retail frames `original/0104`, `0106`):** a pure white 3 x 3 pixel square at the centre of the 1920 x 1080
  frame. Frames before Max has a weapon (`0098`, `0100`) show none.
- **Engine:** draws that square, scaled to the frame, while a weapon with `dot.kf2` is in hand.
- **Not read:** the binary traces a cast (`CastLength = 100`, `SphereRadius`) and places a mesh, so the real dot may move
  or change size with the distance (`ScreenSizeFrontLimit`, `ScreenSizeBackLimit`). Nothing in the tutorials covers it.
